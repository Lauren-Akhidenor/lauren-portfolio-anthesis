The problem I'm exploring: How can agricultural systems become more sustainable, secure and resilient to disruption?

The person behind the question
Who am I?

Beyond who I am based on my academic qualifications and technical skills, I am someone driven more by discipline than motivation, with a strong desire to contribute to the food system in whatever meaningful way I can. I am curious about how agricultural systems work, the pressures they face, and the decisions that shape what happens within them.

What do I do?

I work with agricultural data to turn complex information into evidence that can support better decisions. My approach combines data analysis with economic thinking, looking not only at what the numbers show, but also at why they matter and what they might mean for decisions in practice.

What am I interested in?

I am interested in building food systems that are more secure, sustainable and resilient. For me, that means looking at the whole system: the businesses and farmers who produce food, the people who depend on it, and the natural resources that agriculture both depends on and affects.

I am particularly interested in what happens beyond measuring sustainability, how evidence can help shape strategies and approaches that are practical, economically realistic and resilient to change.

Why did I create this?

I created this portfolio to show how I think about these questions through two data-driven projects. Rather than simply saying what I am interested in, I wanted to make my approach visible — showing how I use evidence, agricultural economics and data analysis to explore problems and think towards practical solutions.

Why Anthesis?

Anthesis' work across agriculture, food systems, circularity and supply-chain resilience closely reflects the direction I want to develop my career. What particularly interests me is the connection between sustainability and what happens in practice: how businesses, farmers and food systems can respond to environmental and economic pressures while becoming more resilient.

That intersection is where I see myself continuing to learn and contribute.

What can I bring, and what do I want to learn?

I bring discipline, curiosity, an understanding of agricultural systems, and a commitment to using evidence to support better decisions. I am not interested in sustainability as reporting alone. Measurement matters, but I am particularly interested in what happens with the evidence afterwards, how it can inform strategies, economic choices and implementation.

I also want to learn from people working on these challenges in practice: how multidisciplinary teams turn evidence into strategy, how sustainability decisions are made within businesses and supply chains, and how those strategies can become both sustainable and resilient in the real world.

Case PL–01

Producing More, Losing More

A data-driven look at global agricultural risk

Why this project

What if producing more food doesn't necessarily mean a food system is less vulnerable?

That question led me to look beyond production volumes and explore what happens across the wider agricultural system, from productivity and post-harvest losses to trade exposure and food availability.

Using secondary agricultural data, I examined these patterns across different food-system contexts. The project also demonstrates how I work with data, from structuring and analysing raw datasets to statistical analysis, predictive modelling and visualisation, to explore where vulnerabilities may exist within agricultural systems.

Case FL–02

The Fuel Lag

Tracing how energy price shocks drive UK food inflation through supply chains

Why this project

What happens when an energy shock doesn't stop at the fuel pump, but travels through the food supply chain?

That question led me to examine how changes in fuel prices are transmitted into UK food inflation, how long that transmission takes, and whether some food categories are more exposed than others.

Using UK price data from 2015–2025, I applied time-series and econometric analysis to trace these relationships and explore what energy-price movements could tell us about food-price pressures.

The project demonstrates how I use data and econometric methods to investigate relationships across interconnected systems and translate complex patterns into insights relevant to food-system resilience and food security planning.

Case OR–03

Operational Resilience at the Farm Level

Automated restock alerts and climate-price volatility analysis, built with SQL Server and Power BI.

Why this project

What if a farm could spot an operational risk before it becomes a bigger problem?

That question led me to look at resilience from the farm-management side: what happens when essential inputs are running low, while uncertainty around crop prices is also building?

I built a small decision-support workflow that brings these two sides together, using SQL Server to structure and monitor farm data, automated restock logic to flag inputs that need attention, and Power BI to bring operational and price information into one view.

The project demonstrates how I use data not just to analyse what has happened, but to build practical ways of identifying where attention may be needed.

Different Problems. One Bigger Question.

Across three projects, I have looked at food-system risk from different angles, farm operations, supply chains and global agricultural systems.

The common thread is simple:

How can we build food systems that are more secure, sustainable and resilient?

From input shortages and price uncertainty at farm level, to energy-driven food inflation and global production, loss and trade risks, each project explores a different pressure within the system.

Food Security × Sustainability × Resilience

These projects have shaped how I think about food-system problems: the risks may look different, but they are connected.

How I Approach a Food-System Problem
01

Understand the problem

I start with the food-system question, not the dataset. I look at where the pressure sits, whether at farm level, across supply chains, or within the wider agricultural system.

02

Build the evidence

I work with agricultural and economic data from secondary sources, structuring and cleaning it so that it can answer the question being asked.

03

Analyse the relationships

I use tools such as Excel, SQL, SPSS, Python and Power BI to identify patterns, relationships, trends and areas of risk.

04

Connect evidence to decisions

I ask what the findings mean beyond the numbers, where a vulnerability may exist, what it could mean for food security or resilience, and where further investigation may be needed.

Why Anthesis?

Systems-Level Resilience in Food & Agriculture

One thing that particularly drew me to Anthesis is the way you approach resilience as part of how food and agricultural systems need to change. Your work in regenerative agriculture connects areas such as soil health, water resilience and farmer livelihoods with the resilience of the wider food supply chain. Bridging the Farm-to-Boardroom Risk Gap

That system-wide way of thinking mirrors how my own interests have developed. I am interested in the connections between pressures at different points in a food system, from resource availability, energy and farm-level production to the supply-chain and business risks that can emerge further downstream.

I want to develop my ability to analyse these interconnected risks from a business perspective and understand where organisations can strengthen their resilience and adapt to change.

I want to move beyond theoretical sustainability and understand how systemic environmental questions become practical business decisions.

Driving Scalable Impact

What also attracts me to Anthesis is the opportunity to work on solutions that can be applied beyond a single project or organisation. From digital approaches to reducing food waste to developing better ways of measuring sustainability, your work shows how data, collaboration and practical tools can support change across value chains.

I want to bring my agricultural and analytical perspective to that environment, while learning how complex food-system and environmental risks can be translated into practical, scalable solutions.
